Privacy

What each part of Rowel can see, stated precisely enough to be checked against the source.

Last updated 3 October 2026.

The short version

  • No accounts. We never ask for your name, email or phone number.
  • Your conversations, code and commands are encrypted end to end between your phone and your Mac. The relay in between cannot decrypt them.
  • The relay keeps no logs and stores almost nothing: which Macs are online, and a pairing code while you use one. Both are deleted when no longer needed.
  • The app has no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no crash reporter that sends anything anywhere. This website counts visits, without cookies — see This website.
  • On the same Wi-Fi the phone usually talks to your Mac directly, and the relay never carries that session.

What the relay can see

The app and Bridle run a Noise IK handshake with each other and then exchange sealed frames. The relay forwards those frames between two sockets by circuit number. It holds no key and cannot read them. It does see:

Machine ID Random, made the first time Bridle runs — the hash of its public key, not derived from your hardware.
Machine name In the clear, so the app can show which Mac it is connecting to. It defaults to your computer's name; change machineName in ~/.rowel/bridle.json if that says more than you would like. Anyone who knows your machine ID can ask the relay for this name and your Bridle version while Bridle is connected.
Bridle version Reported when Bridle connects.
Who talks to whom That some phone opened a circuit to some machine, and when.
Volume and timing Unavoidable for anything that forwards bytes: enough to tell you are busy, not what you are doing.
IP addresses Yours, as with any server you connect to.
Push tokens Your phone's notification token, at the moment a notification is sent — see Notifications.

It never sees your prompts, the model's replies, file contents, diffs, command output, API keys or file paths.

On the same Wi-Fi the app tries your Mac directly and only dials the relay a quarter of a second later; the direct path normally wins, and then the relay never learns that session happened. Bridle itself stays connected to the relay either way, so your Mac is reachable when you are not at home.

During pairing

Pairing by QR code: the invitation goes from your screen to your camera and never passes through the relay. Pairing by typed code (bridle pair --code) puts a pairing bundle on the relay so the app can fetch it: your machine's name, ID, public key, local network addresses, and a one-time token that can only ask to pair — you accept the phone at the Mac. The bundle is kept until a phone enters the code or it expires, 10 minutes at most (the relay refuses to hold one longer than 15).

What the relay stores

The relay is a Cloudflare Worker. The objects that carry your traffic write nothing to storage; while a connection is open, Cloudflare holds its machine ID, name, version and circuit numbers alongside it, and drops them when it closes. What is written to storage:

While a Mac is online One row: its machine ID, name, Bridle version, when it connected, and how many phones are attached. Deleted when it disconnects; a sweep every ten minutes removes rows whose connection went without a clean close.
While a typed code is outstanding The pairing bundle above, and a note of which codes that machine has open and when they lapse. Deleted when the code is used or expires.
Once The time the relay first started. Nothing about you.

Not your messages, not who talked to whom, not a history of how much or when. Cloudflare keeps its own 30-day recovery log for this kind of storage, which code could use to roll it back to an earlier moment; the relay has no code that does, and we do not use it.

Logs are switched off. The relay's code prints a line in two cases: when a machine reconnects while its previous connection is still registered (a laptop waking up, or two Bridles fighting over one machine ID — the line includes the machine ID), and when a notification fails to send. Those lines are visible only to someone watching the live stream at that moment. Nothing is kept.

Cloudflare

The relay runs at rowel-relay.novabox.ai, on its own hostname, separate from this site. Cloudflare terminates TLS and runs the code, so it can see the same metadata as the relay — IP addresses, timing, volume — under its own privacy policy. It cannot read your traffic: what it forwards is sealed between your phone and your Mac.

What stays on your devices

On the Mac

Under ~/.rowel, Bridle keeps its identity keys, the phones it has paired (their names, keys and notification tokens), any pairing request waiting for your approval, and — if you installed the background service — a log, bridle.log, that records connections by device name. Your conversations stay where the DeepSeek Harness keeps them; Bridle does not copy them to disk.

On the phone

The app keeps its identity key in the iOS Keychain. In its own sandboxed storage: the list of paired Macs, the name this iPhone reports, which Mac it was last on, per-Mac display settings (default model, which workspace sections are folded), the lock settings, and any hang or crash diagnostics iOS hands the app — kept on the phone, and sent only if you share one yourself.

Your conversations are not stored on the phone. The session list and messages are fetched from your Mac and held in memory; closing the app discards them. The exception is the notifications the app posts while it is still connected in the background — a question's text, the name of a tool asking permission, or “Finished”, with the conversation's title and the Mac's name — which stay in Notification Center until you clear them.

Forgetting a Mac in the app is one-sided: the phone forgets the machine, but the Mac keeps trusting the phone until you run bridle revoke there. bridle devices shows what it still trusts.

The lock on the app

A paired iPhone can approve commands on your Mac, so Rowel locks itself by default. It asks for Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode on launch, and when you come back after being away longer than a delay you choose (one minute unless you change it). It covers the screen the moment the app leaves the foreground, so the app switcher shows a lock, not your work. You can turn it off, and it is off on a phone with no passcode.

It narrows the window; it does not close it. If you lose the phone, revoke it on the Mac with bridle revoke — there is no way to do that from another phone yet.

Notifications

When your agent stops to ask and no phone is connected to that Mac, the Mac has the relay send a notification to every phone paired with it that has notifications on. Apple only accepts pushes signed with a developer key, so the relay signs them — which makes it the one part of this that learns your device token. The token is handed over one notification at a time and not stored.

The push is the whole notification, and it carries no question. It is titled “Rowel” and reads “Your agent is waiting on you. · name”, with your Mac's name (cut to 64 characters) so someone with two Macs knows which one stopped. The sentence is fixed in the relay's source; the message your Mac sends has no field an agent's question could go in. The push does not run the app. When you open Rowel, it connects over its own encrypted tunnel and shows you the actual request — text that never crossed the relay and never reached Apple.

So Apple's push service knows that one of your Macs wanted your attention, and when — not what about. The relay knows which device token belongs to which machine.

To stop notifications, turn them off for Rowel in iOS Settings. The next time the app opens it tells the Mac it connects to to forget the token, and other paired Macs forget it when the app next connects to them. A Mac you forgot only on the phone keeps notifying it until you run bridle revoke there.

This website

rowel.novabox.ai counts visits with PostHog: which pages are viewed, and clicks such as joining the TestFlight or copying the install command. No cookies — a random visit id lives in the tab's session storage and is gone when you close the tab. No session recording. The script is served from this site, and its reports go through this site too, which passes on neither your IP address nor cookies; the country, from Cloudflare, is the only location recorded. None of this applies to the app, which sends nothing to PostHog or anyone else.

Changes

Material changes are made here, with a new date at the top. The relay keeps no record of who uses it, so there is no list to notify — this page is the only place changes appear.

Checking any of this

Everything above is in the source. The production relay's entire state is in relay-worker/src/exchange.ts, pair-code.ts and switchboard.ts (the self-hostable Node relay's is in relay/src/registry.ts and offers.ts, plus in-memory rate limits per IP). An end-to-end test, the relay only ever sees ciphertext (e2e/tests/security.test.js), taps the phone's traffic through the Node relay and fails if a method name, a session id, the machine's name or a conversation title appears in it; it runs wherever a DeepSeek Harness is available.
